# Independent adversarial audit — role-proof ticket resale

**Audit date:** 12 November 2026  
**Object:** `role-proof-audit-2026-11-11(1).zip` (`gre_sim.py`, `role_proof.py`, data and 11 November revision of `POLICY_BRIEF.md`).  
**Classification key:** **Wrong** = arithmetic, implementation or factual error; **Unjustified** = evidence does not support asserted conclusion; **Arguable** = a defensible alternative changes inference.  
**Scope:** code and document inspection; 14 automated tests; stored-output checks; independent simulation experiments; verification of selected primary legal and journal sources. Not a field validation or full end-to-end rerun of all 121,000 role-switch simulations and 400 Latin-hypercube experiments.

## Overall judgment

**Do not publish the brief as presently written.** The author's baseline **within-model** comparison of a 20–30% cap with fee-capped face value does reproduce directionally; this is not a discovery that all results are fabricated or wrong. But the central claim of economically marginal high-demand touting collapses under a small defensible dark-market repricing. A joint scenario involving frictionless fan listing and no positive option-utility score reverses the headline mean-fan comparison. The recommended 20–30% cap has *not* passed its own formal role-proofness test. The apparently strong 400-draw robustness figure is the ex-post best of three ceilings, not a fixed ceiling. The Ontario and D.C. law descriptions contain correctable errors.

## 2. Direct independent experiments

### A. High-demand dark prices: touting return reverses

Independent harness `stress_audit.py`, results `stress_audit.csv`, **120 seeded runs per scenario**. Shared seeds for the three fee-capped resale ceilings; base program is imported, not edited on disk. The alternative is a **single-line dark ask markdown** to `min(original_dark_price, $400)`, where $400 is the highest configured fan WTP. This is deliberately the most conservative useful change to dark sellers' behavior: market participants seek a price a buyer can pay instead of stubbornly insisting on an unmarketable quote. No empirical claim about the true clearing price is made.

| Demand | Cap | Baseline dark tickets sold/event | Baseline ROI | ROI after dark discount-to-WTP | Mean-fan advantage vs face with discount |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5× | 20% | 4.67 | +9.2% | **+25.9%** | +0.01406 |
| 5× | 30% | 4.12 | +18.0% | **+32.8%** | +0.01825 |
| 7× | 20% | 0 | −4.6% | **+31.3%** | +0.00828 |
| 7× | 30% | 0 | +5.0% | **+38.0%** | +0.01066 |
| 10× | 20% | 0 | −9.8% | **+36.2%** | +0.00564 |
| 10× | 30% | 0 | −1.0% | **+42.6%** | +0.00731 |

These profits are *modelled net of the chosen 45% dark friction*, but not independently measured operating costs. The alternative preserves the *direction* of C1 while **breaking the central scalper-profit proposition**. Seller revenue and fan spending would change further with transaction-specific bargaining, variable fraud cost and alternative WTP distributions. The official model cannot claim to have established no viable black market merely because its ask generator quotes beyond buyer budgets.

### B. An interaction overturns the average-fan comparison

Independent harness `audit_targeted.py` with **250 paired seeds per cell**: fixed 5%/side fee; `fan_listing='always'`; resale-option weight set to zero; remaining utility weights renormalized from `.35,.30,.20,.15` to `.35/.85,.30/.85,.20/.85,0`. This is an **alternative normative scoring lens**, not a code correction. The script also runs the original listing and weights as a control. The two changes had been probed individually in the author's earlier work, but not in this interaction. P0 = face-price score saturates at 2×; P2 = price relative to personal reservation value.

| Demand | Price form | Baseline 20% − face | Joint 20% − face (95% Monte Carlo CI) | Baseline 30% − face | Joint 30% − face (95% Monte Carlo CI) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3× | P2 | +0.02315 | **−0.00258** [−0.00287, −0.00229] | +0.03035 | **−0.00464** [−0.00536, −0.00393] |
| 7× | P0 | +0.00808 | **−0.00233** [−0.00245, −0.00221] | +0.01041 | **−0.00372** [−0.00385, −0.00360] |
| 7× | P2 | +0.00703 | **−0.00353** [−0.00366, −0.00340] | +0.00884 | **−0.00554** [−0.00568, −0.00540] |
| 10× | P0 | +0.00532 | **−0.00184** [−0.00189, −0.00179] | +0.00693 | **−0.00280** [−0.00286, −0.00274] |

The result is not “the real-world optimal cap is zero”; it is a model-internal **robustness counterexample**, and therefore contradicts an unqualified claim that reasonable alternative assumptions cannot reverse the conclusion. Confidence intervals represent random-seed precision *conditional on exactly these assumptions*, and do not measure epistemic uncertainty over the assumed market mechanisms.

### C. Baseline check and numerical caveats

- 14/14 unit tests passed: `python -m unittest test_invariants test_role_proof -q`.
- `python price_check.py --smoke`: first P0 stored-grid check passed, two-seed variant mechanics exercised.
- `python price_check.py --verdicts`: **0 mismatches** across the stored **450 P0 grid and 220 P0 role-proof** comparisons. P1/P2 verdicts were recomputed from supplied CSVs, **not** an independent 121,000-run re-execution.
- C1 original 300-paired-seed logs (`brief_claims_check.log`): P0 mean-fan difference **20%-face** at 1.5× +0.0462; at 10× +0.0053. For **30%-face** at 1.5× +0.0951; at 10× +0.0069. All reported mean-fan 95% CIs exclude zero for P0/P1/P2 under original conditions.
- **Numerically overstated interpretation:** at 10×, 30%-face **broader population-weighted composite** P0 +0.0000 with CI **[−0.0022,+0.0022]**; P2 −0.0016 with CI **[−0.0038,+0.0006]**. So the composite does **not** support a statistically positive 30% gain at the extreme, despite positive average fan utility. The brief sometimes acknowledges divergent lenses but rhetoric is more categorical.
- A different comparator, the **uncapped status quo**, actually beats 20% and 30% in average fan utility at 6–10× under P2 (e.g. 20%: 6× −0.0078, 10× −0.0014; 30%: 6× −0.0054, 10× −0.0002 in `brief_claims_check.log`). This does not disprove the **face-value** comparison; it matters to claims of general policy superiority.
- C5 `rescore-paired.csv` S2 / `arm='C5 tough face value, fees 10%/side'`: 10%-face welfare index differences positive at all demand levels, from +0.01085 (1.5×) to +0.02030 (10×); 95% CIs positive, but the fee case is not D.C. law as implemented.

## 4. Five distinctive elements and other assumptions

| Mechanism | Assessment | Most damaging defensible alternative | Observed/testable effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Role-proofness** | Operationalized rigorously as chosen minimax regret, but not as claimed individual or all-participant acceptability; excludes scalpers and the proposed fee package. | Individual veto/positive preference, interim-IR, or finite-tolerance Pareto requirement; include all roles and fee package. | Formal 10–20% test already 0/9; 40% under P1 only. Changed measure can change result. |
| **Adaptive scalpers (“wolves”)** | Entry/exit/markup mechanisms are implemented, but routing has a net-vs-gross bug, and high-demand dark channel is effectively shut by asking too much. | Downward dark-market price adjustment to actual buyer WTP, variable operating costs, competition and dynamic learning. | **30% 10× ROI −1.0% -> +42.6%** in 120-seed test; dark sales 0 -> ~25/event. |

| **Endowment effect** | κ changes only the resale-option weight for a primary ticket holder, not reference-dependent loss from relinquishment. | Kőszegi–Rabin expectation-based reference point; model tickets as an endowed good and regret for missed buyers. | κ had little effect on role-proof verdict; no credible empirical estimate of κ. |
| **Oracle enforcement** | A deliberate upper bound for on-platform compliance, not a real system. ## 5. Method and inference

The paper's correction history is a strength as documentation but does not automatically create confirmatory evidence. The 3 November role-proof test was prospectively defined and **failed**. The 9 November rescoring test found that the previous “10–30% optimal” and “stronger lever 2.13×” claims were false. The price-score amendment R2/C8 was documented **during** a run, reportedly before results were opened; we have not independently authenticated timestamps. Those claims, too, failed the stated cross-score criterion. The final 20–30% band, comparison against fee-capped face, selection of mean-fan utility as leading lens, and associated 300-seed probes are *exploratory following the failures*. They deserve to be reported but not treated as confirmatory out-of-sample estimates. The switch from the flawed welfare composite toward individual fan utility can be a **substantively defensible correction**, since an index that rewards lower inequality by making everyone worse off is a weak objective; however, it becomes lens-shopping when the final objective is selected after learning which lens preserves the preferred policy. Fix by freezing a principled welfare objective and replicating with holdout parameter/market environments rather than just fresh random seeds of the same model.

Minimax-regret is a normative choice, not a universal acceptability condition. A rule with substantial positive role regret may pass because it is **not worse than the alternatives under the chosen aggregation**, and an agent within a role may strictly reject what its average endorses. The all-party wording should either be softened or operationalized with individual acceptance constraints. The half-formed comparison to the UK face-value-only bill cannot be called a passed role-proofness test for fee-capped 20–30%.

## 6. As a UK committee adviser

The strongest potentially persuasive argument is narrow: *under a 5%-per-side secondary fee cap, a fixed 20–30% markup rule scores slightly higher in average fan utility than an identical-conditions face-value rule in this simulated environment*. At 10× the baseline 20%-face difference is +0.0053 utility units and the 30%-face difference +0.0069, small relative to the mean; no pounds-per-fan welfare or enforcement cost is estimated. The draft claims a leap from mechanistic result to a bill recommendation. The fee cap isn't specified as 5% each side by UK policy; it is a key private calibration. The UK government's policy explicitly includes original purchase **total cost** and verification cooperation, not an invented universal registry. The 20% official displacement input may be speculative, but the private model's 45% dark enforcement friction, zero high-demand dark sales and listing response are equally speculative. Observed Ontario resale migration is anecdotal; D.C. and Ontario are not correctly specified legal benchmarks. The 40%/P1 outcome, 0/9 original role-proof failure and joint-counterfactual reversals need prominent presentation, not just a footnote.

The final declaration of “no financial interest in any primary or secondary ticketing business” is narrower than would be necessary if the author has or expects a direct or indirect role in the price-of-record, identity, barcode, registrar, implementation or consulting system that the recommendation presupposes. No such conflict has been independently identified. The declaration should explicitly address any **actual** payments, future ownership/equity, proposals, grant funding, and implementation roles as appropriate, without alleging interests not evidenced.

## 7. Prior art: verified exact references

- **Miguel A. Costa-Gomes, Yuan Ju and Jiawen Li** (2019), “Role-Reversal Consistency: An Experimental Study of the Golden Rule,” *Economic Inquiry* **57(1):685–704**, DOI **10.1111/ecin.12708**. This is the closest *named* verified role-switch consistency concept; it does **not** directly combine ex-ante ignorance, post-allocation endowment shifts, political persistence and an ABM. Qualify novelty as a synthesis/extension, not an unknown concept with no close predecessor.

Canonical idea of ex ante selected policy failing to remain optimal ex post; not synonymous with role-proofness.

Estimated allocative gain ~5% with one-third eroded by combined rent-seeking **and** resale transaction costs. Cite accurately.

## 9. Not checked and limits

- Did **not** execute the entire 121,000-run role-proof experiment, complete 16×10×100 sweep, whole 400-draw 33-dimensional LHS, or every author's 300-seed paired script end to end; those run for tens of minutes and the supplied logs/CSVs were examined instead. Full P1/P2 role-proof results and complete LHS parameter mapping are **stored-data checks, not independently re-executed outputs**.
- Did **not** reconstruct an empirical observation-level model of real-world dark-market clearing, enforcement, purchasing, WTP distributions, queue technologies, presales, gifting or platform seller behavior; our counterfactuals are software stress tests, not calibrated predictions.
- Did **not** independently authenticate pre-registration/amendment creation timestamps, reported original experiments, original Pollstar and Global News displacement amounts, exact tax/fees for all Ontario/DC edge cases, or true author finances/conflicts. These are **not verified** except where confirmed by cited primary legal pages.
- CIs in independent reruns assume independent seeds under a *fixed model*, not parameter calibration error or policy implementation uncertainty. Paired seeds are used for comparisons but rare-event and correlation structure are not extrapolated to real ticket markets.
- Original archived S0 scoring has a **known previous fan-price allocation error**; none of its S0 numbers are being treated as corrected fan welfare. The corrected P0/P1/P2 and S1/S2 output should be distinguished from legacy S0 data.
