Independent adversarial audit: Role-proof ticket-resale model v2
Audit of role-proof-final-audit-2026-11-15.zip
Date: 17 November 2026
Decision: Publish only after specified corrections. The mechanical v2 fixes and baseline run-level results largely reproduce; no discovered new arithmetic error reverses B1. The fixed 20–30% ceiling is nevertheless a conditional policy choice, not an established role-proof optimum. Its advertised upper limit depends materially on uncalibrated dark-market costs and seller fees.

1. Scope and independent verification
- Read the supplied audit brief, v2 assumptions ledger, pre-registration, policy brief, source notes, correction history, core simulation (gre_sim.py), scoring (role_proof.py), experiment (v2_experiment.py), verdicts (v2_verdicts.py), and tests.
- Re-ran 6/6 tests in test_v2.py and 8/8 tests in test_invariants.py, both passing. An attempt at the complete 20-test run was not completed because test_role_proof.PrimaryAllocationIsFixed.test_allocation_identical_across_continuations ran much longer than the audit budget; the 11 earlier cases in that attempt passed, but the complete suite cannot be claimed as independently passed.
- Recomputed v2_verdicts.py from all 62,100 stored rows in nine .csv.gz shards; stdout was byte-identical to v2_verdicts.txt. Checked 207 (mechanics × listing × arm × demand) cells of exactly 300 seeds each.
- Independently regenerated 138 actual simulation runs (all 23 arms × both seeds × three selected demand levels), comparing 10,626 CSV fields: zero differences from shipped data. The full 62,100 simulations were not rerun from first principles.
- Independently compared 160 v1 default mechanics replays between old and new code; no differences. The author's claim of a full 480-run comparison and cap_sweep.py byte-identity was not independently duplicated in its entirety.
- Ran 2,700 fresh v2 stress simulations: four dark-market assumptions × demands 4×/5×/10× × five fixed caps × 45 seeds. Ran another 2,700 fresh simulations: two seller/buyer fee levels (2%, 10%) × two fan-listing regimes × demands 3×/5×/10× × five caps × 45 seeds. These are exploratory post-audit simulations, not part of the author's pre-registration.
- Checked current Ontario and UK official legislative materials, D.C. legislation and selected academic citations. These external sources are independent of the package.
Interpretation caution: intervals on the authors' verdicts and Monte Carlo comparisons represent conditional simulation variation, not an uncertainty distribution over plausible real-world behavioral parameters. The new independent stress tests have only 45 paired seeds per point.

Special note on implementation status
The new M1 dark clearing, M1b expectation, M2 net-vs-net routing, M3 all-in buyer affordability, and M4 below-face fan markdown changes implement the stated design and pass the targeted unit tests. I found no new demonstrated low-level code bug of comparable seriousness to the original F01, F05, F09 or F10. N05 is a behavioral-model alternative, not a violation of the stated implementation. The full simulation is nevertheless not economically validated by internal test passing.

3. Claim-by-claim verdicts B1–B10
B1 Positive caps 10–40% beat face for fans in S1/S2 at all 9 demands across P0/P1/P2 — Holds with caveat. Independently reproduced verdicts from all stored run data; 138 individual simulations replayed exactly. This is a within-model result conditional on stylized scoring and behavior; not evidence of UK population welfare.
B2 20% fan mean +4–11%; 30% +7–24% — Holds with caveat. Independent aggregation: about 4.21–11.19% and 6.94–24.22% in prescribed S1/S2/P0 samples. These are percentage changes in a normalized constructed index, not 4–24% cash saving or welfare in pounds.
B3 At 5× casual/average access 4% -> 7% -> 8%; lawful resales 86 -> 135 -> 156 — Holds. v2_verdicts.txt Reported, S1, demand 5: 4/4, 7/7, 8/8 percent, lawful 85.9, 134.6, 156.2.
B4 ROI, fairness and off-platform shares — Holds with major caveat. Exact supplied baseline means reproduce; model-defined 30% fairness fails under reasonable cost changes; 40% passes ROI at 10%-per-side fees in sampled demands. "Today's-market" ROI is model-implied and index-capped.
B5 30% beats 20% for fans at every level (S1/S2 primary score) — Holds. All 9 P0 paired differences positive, 95% CI above zero; e.g. S1 +0.0513 at 1.5× and +0.0023 at 10×. Sensitivity to scoring/behavior noted in report.
B6 20–30% fail role-proof; 10% alone passes S1 — Holds with caveat. Correct under author-defined detection-based group-average metric, not universal consent or statistical non-inferiority. The actual proposed band fails even that metric.
B7 Band fails only in S4 — Holds with wording caveat. Among the four defined experiments, only S4 fails the all-level Q1 criterion. Yet in S4/P0 cap30 retains significant positive gains at several demands, and outside the four scenarios a lower dark-enforcement cost breaks its fairness bound. "Only" cannot mean all imaginable alternatives.
B8 Face-value cap beats today at most demand levels — Holds with caveat. Stored paired contrasts confirm 2–10× under S1/S2; at 1.5× face loses under S1. Today's-market 2× comparison is known to be distorted by uncapped broker markdowns.
B9 Ceiling ranking does not change with demand — Fails as written; holds in primary scenarios. Accurate for primary S1/S2 B/P0; false without qualification in S4/P2 (face vs 30% ranking reverses between 1.5× and 3×).
B10 Legal/prior-art sources — Fails in part. D.C., Leslie–Sorensen and Costa-Gomes et al. appear corrected; Ontario Bill 97/Sch.16 and allowed secondary fees still misstated or omitted; Ireland causal claim unjustified; UK "draft Bill as written" premature without actual text.

C. Mechanics regression
- No dark ask exceeds the buyer's WTP in v2's specified transaction mechanism; test_v2.py confirms the midpoint of seller reservation and buyer limit for observed sales.
- On-platform buyers must satisfy all-in affordability (gre_sim.py:650, 683).
- Platform-vs-dark seller comparison uses seller-fee-net values (gre_sim.py:276–286, 549–551).
- Fan markdown removes v1's face floor (gre_sim.py:671–676).
- v1 default unchanged in the tested 160 old-vs-new paired simulations; the full author's byte-identity test wasn't independently repeated.
D. Pre-registration, inference and policy meaning
V2_PREREGISTRATION.md:90–100 faithfully specifies D1–D3, and v2_verdicts.py:178–192 implements them. The new experiment has a tighter protocol than the previous exploratory sequence. Nonetheless the protocol's conditional design cannot transform uncalibrated behavior into external validity; the D1 rule's headline is driven by the authors' chosen average-utility metric and selected numeric ceilings rather than a role-proof acceptance threshold. Its name should not be used to imply empirical consensus.

6. Three changes required before publication
1. Correct the claims and legal references without another optimization loop. Cite Ontario Bill 97, Schedule 16, including permitted secondary fees and taxes; remove the Ireland enforcement causal claim; replace unpublished "draft Bill as written" with "Government's announced proposals" unless actual draft text is found. Clarify baseline-only demand ranking and the nuanced S4 results.
2. Publish the dark-cost and fee-cap sensitivity table immediately alongside the 20–30% claim. Say the upper bound is a normative and model-dependent cap on reseller returns, not a robust result. Say the fan-welfare direction survived the specific additional cost/fee counterfactuals tested, not arbitrary plausible combinations.
3. Align the role-proof branding with what the test actually measured. Describe 10% as a rule with no statistically detected group-average disadvantage in S1, not "a rule no group objects to." State explicitly that the 20–30% recommendation sacrifices the model's role-proofness condition for a greater modeled fan-average gain. If stronger role-proof claims are intended, noninferiority margins and actual participant preference elicitation would be necessary.

7. Publication call
PUBLISH WITH LISTED WORDING AND EVIDENCE FIXES, NOT AS WRITTEN. The v2 run-level results are reproducible and most original mechanical bugs appear closed. A measured conditional recommendation for a positive resale ceiling can be circulated as a simulation-based policy argument, not a causal welfare estimate. The document should not call 20–30% a uniquely robust role-proof interval, and it should not call 30% universally "fairly governed" on the strength of a 45% dark cost and a 5%-per-side fee assumption. No further large, iterative search for a preferred ceiling is required for a clearly exploratory working paper; legislative prescription would demand stronger empirical validation.

8. Checks not completed
- Did not independently run the entire 62,100-simulation original v2 design; matched every stored verdict and 138 fully regenerated runs instead.
- Did not run the complete 20-test suite to completion; the original role-proof primary-allocation roundtrip was too expensive in this environment. The complete 14 original-unit-test pass is author-reported, while the 8 original invariants and 6 new v2 tests were independently verified.
- Did not fully repeat cap_sweep.py CSV byte regeneration or the complete 480-run v1 cross-version comparison; independently checked 160 paired simulations.
- Did not establish that 45% off-platform enforcement loss, 5%-per-side platform fees, or midpoint bargaining describe British resale markets. No transaction-level calibration data was provided.
- Did not independently authenticate the Nov. 15 internal protocol's creation time, government publication status of a final draft Bill, ROI outside the chosen costs, or a causal effect from Ontario and Ireland.
- Did not test every joint interaction (e.g. demand-dependent eligibility × fee × bargaining × fraud × competing sellers) or real-world enforcement recordkeeping cost. Results from unsampled assumptions must not be inferred.
